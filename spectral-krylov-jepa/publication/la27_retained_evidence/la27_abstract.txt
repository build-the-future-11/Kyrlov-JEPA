We examine a retained study of spectral Krylov pretraining for quantum ground-state prediction. A joint embedding predictor learns from short Lanczos trajectories, then transfers a potential encoder to energy and wavefunction regression. The study uses a 16 by 16 interior grid, one training seed, twelve in-distribution Gaussian-well test potentials, and twenty labels. Mean fidelity and residual at the true energy are 0.991650 and 40.190890 for Krylov pretraining, 0.994460 and 1.483805 for a fixed free-box state, and 0.999898 and 0.761666 for a nine-mode Rayleigh-Ritz baseline. Rayleigh-Ritz uses the query Hamiltonian and has a different inference cost; this comparison does not establish equal-compute superiority. The paired Krylov-minus-free-box fidelity interval includes zero. These exploratory comparisons condition on one fitted model and exclude training-seed uncertainty. The retained result illustrates how high overlap can conceal substantial residual error, and how classical subspace references make a learned solver evaluation more informative. Useful transfer and label efficiency remain unestablished; the larger confirmatory experiment has not run. We present the retained comparison and its limits, with no new optimization, outcome sweep, or protected evaluation.
