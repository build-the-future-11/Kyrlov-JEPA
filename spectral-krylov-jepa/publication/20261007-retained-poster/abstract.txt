A parsimonious classical model can expose an uninformative test of representation learning. We examine a retained study of spectral Krylov pretraining for quantum ground states. A joint embedding predictor learns from Lanczos trajectories and transfers the potential encoder to energy and wavefunction prediction. The transfer hypothesis remains open; executable machinery alone does not establish label efficiency.

The retained study used a grid with sixteen interior points per axis, one training seed, and twelve in distribution test potentials generated from Gaussian wells. At twenty labels, Krylov pretraining achieved mean fidelity 0.991650 and residual at the true energy 40.190890. A fixed free box wavefunction achieved fidelity 0.994460 and residual 1.483805. A nine mode Rayleigh Ritz baseline achieved 0.999898 and 0.761666, respectively. The latter uses the query Hamiltonian and has a different inference cost profile; these comparisons do not establish equal compute superiority.

High overlap therefore concealed substantial physical error on this small distribution. The paired Krylov minus free box fidelity interval includes zero, while comparison with the nine mode baseline favors the baseline within this retained corpus. These intervals are exploratory, conditional on one fitted model, and exclude training seed uncertainty.

We present the adverse comparison, a distinction between fidelity and residual accuracy, and a classical reference that makes the evaluation interpretable. The larger confirmatory experiment has not run. No new optimization, outcome sweep, or protected evaluation is included. The presentation motivates distribution difficulty, physics checks, and matched cost accounting before treating a high fidelity neural result as evidence of useful transfer.
